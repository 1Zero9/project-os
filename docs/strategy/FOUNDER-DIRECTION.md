# Project OS — Founder Direction

**Type:** Founder direction
**Status:** Active
**Evidence classification:** Statement of intent, not experimental evidence
**Project OS v0.1:** Remains frozen
**Framework effect:** None

---

## The beginning

Project OS started because I was repeatedly designing and building ideas from a blank page.

An idea could be for almost anything: an app, a dashboard, a website, a community tool or something created purely for fun. I would often begin with the branding, choosing a name, colour scheme, icon or logo before working out the product itself. Every project began as a new piece of work, even when I had already solved similar problems elsewhere.

That approach produced some successes, but it also resulted in projects that failed, stalled or never quite hit the mark. I tend to get caught in minor tweaks, colour changes and repeated attempts to find something that feels right. That consumes time and AI tokens, creates frustration and can distract me from the problem I originally wanted to solve.

I am not a traditional software developer. My ability to build has developed alongside AI. My journey started with Google searches and novelty images, moved into prompting, and eventually progressed to creating dashboards, applications, websites and working products. AI gave me the ability to do things that would previously have required a development team, but it also made it possible to create more ideas and projects than I could properly manage.

Project OS is the next stage of that journey.

The aim is to learn from everything I have already built and everything I build in the future. Each project should improve the next one. I should not have to return to a blank page when useful decisions, patterns, components and lessons already exist.

This is also the evolution of 1Zero9 from a personal brand into a service I can deliver independently: a human CEO working alongside an AI workforce to produce things for fun, for communities or for profit.

A better way of working means using accelerators to move quickly through the foundations while applying standards, security controls and practical guardrails. Speed matters, but the result also needs to be something I am prepared to stand over.

## The discovery

I do not yet know whether Project OS has achieved what I ultimately want from it.

The six experiments have produced evidence and useful lessons, but I still need to see something tangible. We built part of the LaunchCity website but did not finish it. We explored a golf handicap tool without developing the product. We also examined previous projects and tested whether knowledge could transfer between them.

That work suggests potential, but there is still a considerable distance between a documented framework and the operating system I have in mind.

I want the accumulated knowledge to keep growing until Project OS can act as both my project manager and my project lead. In time, I want to be able to give it an idea, work with it to clarify the opportunity, and then allow it to organise the work.

Depending on the idea, that could mean producing the application, or developing the plans, prompts, decisions and stages required to build it. The target is to reach roughly 80% completion using what has been learned from past projects, what is known about the current project and what Project OS continues to learn in the future.

The experiments have helped establish foundations. They have not yet proven the full vision. The next test is whether those foundations can produce something complete, useful and recognisably better than starting again from scratch.

## The ambition

The ambition is to build a business around 1Zero9.

That business would provide useful, bespoke and affordable AI-enabled products and services. It would build new tools, improve existing ones, maintain them and continue delivering value after the initial launch.

I see myself as the human founder and CEO, supported by an AI workforce covering the different capabilities a small technology company needs. These could include research, product management, design, development, security, governance, testing, infrastructure, marketing, sales, maintenance and customer support.

Over time, these functions could develop their own identities, names, responsibilities and communication channels under the 1Zero9 brand. From a client's perspective, they would experience a coherent company and service. I would remain accountable for the work, the decisions and the quality of what 1Zero9 delivers.

Project OS would be the system coordinating that company. It would retain its knowledge, direct the work, apply its standards and help decide what to build, reuse, improve, maintain, pause or stop.

## The bigger possibility

Ideas are the spark.

The golf handicap tool is one example. A problem identified in one golf club could become a tool for other clubs. The same thinking could then extend into football, tennis, padel, fishing or other sports.

I have already started several projects in the sports and community space. Viewed separately, they are individual ideas. Viewed as a portfolio, they could become shared products, reusable services or the foundations of a wider sports website and club-management network, perhaps serving some of the same needs as platforms such as ClubZap.

The opportunity is wider than sport.

It might be a macOS application that continues supporting Intel processors. It might be a better screenshot utility. It might be a small product that solves one frustrating problem well. It could also mean identifying local businesses without a useful internet presence and offering them affordable websites, digital tools and ongoing maintenance.

I want an R&D capability within 1Zero9 that continually examines problems, industries and underserved groups. Its role would be to find ideas, assess whether they have value, investigate what already exists and propose projects worth pursuing.

Project OS could eventually make that repeatable. An idea would enter the system, be researched and challenged, and then be developed, tested, parked or rejected based on its value and feasibility.

## Knowing when to intervene

The company idea changes how I think about intervention.

I imagine a team meeting where different perspectives come together to discuss an idea, challenge assumptions, explore risks and agree on a plan. The team might be made up of AI roles, agents or tools, but the exact technology and methodology matter less than the quality of the decisions they help me make.

A pre-mortem could be triggered before a significant commitment. The team would assume that the project failed and work backwards to understand why. That could expose a weak idea, missing requirement, security concern, unrealistic scope or lack of a clear user before too much effort has been invested.

A post-mortem could be triggered after a launch, failure, major decision, incident or abandoned project. Its purpose would be to capture what happened, what should change and what future projects could reuse.

These interventions should happen when they add value. Project OS should recognise when a project is drifting, when repeated design changes are replacing progress, when scope is expanding, when a decision carries real risk or when useful learning is about to be lost.

The goal is to create a smarter way of working: using the knowledge, tools and experience already available to build secure, high-quality products with the best AI technologies available at the time.

Project OS should make the company more capable with every project it completes.
