# ADR 001 — Project OS v0.1 Promotions

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision ID:** 001
- **Date:** 2026-09-14
- **Evidence:** `docs/archaeology/001-lucy.md` through `007-scribble.md`, plus `docs/archaeology/SYNTHESIS-001.md`
- **Scope:** Define the smallest set of capabilities promoted into Project OS v0.1

---

## 1. Context

Project OS began as an attempt to make future projects benefit from the decisions, failures, patterns and experience of previous projects.

The initial risk was obvious: because almost any useful-sounding process, agent, skill, template or automation could plausibly belong in such a system, Project OS could become a framework built from speculation rather than evidence.

To avoid that, the first phase used archaeology across seven materially different projects:

1. Lucy
2. Eolas
3. U12 Scorekeeper
4. Rivervalley Rangers Website
5. IMS Exception Process
6. Ground Control
7. Scribble

`SYNTHESIS-001.md` compared those projects and identified findings that survived cross-project comparison.

This ADR converts that evidence into a bounded decision for Project OS v0.1.

The question is not:

> What could Project OS contain?

It is:

> **What is the minimum operating capability that our evidence says Project OS should contain now?**

---

## 2. Decision

Project OS v0.1 will promote **five lightweight capabilities**:

1. **Project Intake**
2. **Relevant Knowledge Check**
3. **Risk & Failure Check**
4. **Scope & Stop Check**
5. **Learning Capture**

These are capabilities, not implementation forms.

This decision does **not** yet prescribe that they become:

- agents;
- skills;
- commands;
- scripts;
- applications;
- services;
- automated workflows.

The first implementation should use the least machinery capable of testing whether these five capabilities improve a real project.

---

# 3. Promotion 1 — Project Intake

## Decision

**PROMOTE**

Project OS v0.1 will include a lightweight Project Intake capability.

## Purpose

Establish enough context to understand the project and determine how much process it deserves.

## Minimum questions

Project Intake should establish, where relevant:

- What problem or job are we trying to address?
- Who is affected?
- What already exists?
- What existing behaviour or system must be understood?
- What constraints are material?
- What decisions have already been made?
- What is uncertain?
- What is expensive or dangerous to get wrong?
- How reversible are the likely decisions?
- How much process is justified?

## Evidence

The archaeology repeatedly showed that different projects need different treatment.

- Lucy had high product/design uncertainty.
- Scorekeeper had a clear operational job.
- Rivervalley introduced public and reputational consequences.
- IMS introduced governance, security and audit consequences.
- Ground Control had broad possibilities but low household risk.
- Scribble required strong boundaries without heavyweight ceremony.

## Important constraint

Project Intake must not become a universal questionnaire that every project completes in full.

Its job is to determine what matters, not to create documentation for its own sake.

## v0.1 hypothesis

A short adaptive intake can prevent premature solution generation while remaining cheaper than correcting a badly framed project later.

---

# 4. Promotion 2 — Relevant Knowledge Check

## Decision

**PROMOTE**

Project OS v0.1 will include a Relevant Knowledge Check.

## Purpose

Prevent projects from unnecessarily starting from zero.

Before significant decisions are reinvented, check whether previous work already provides useful evidence.

## Relevant knowledge may include

- previous decisions;
- architecture choices;
- failure lessons;
- operational incidents;
- product patterns;
- anti-patterns;
- implementation examples;
- known constraints;
- previous trade-offs.

## Evidence

Eolas directly exposed the loss of knowledge between projects.

Other archaeology strengthened the case:

- Scorekeeper showed leverage from established technical choices.
- Rivervalley produced reusable production/DNS knowledge.
- IMS produced reusable workflow and governance lessons.
- Lucy produced reusable design/process failure patterns.

## Important constraint

The check is for **relevant** knowledge.

Project OS must not force reuse merely because something similar exists.

Previous knowledge is evidence, not authority.

## v0.1 hypothesis

A small manual retrieval step before important decisions will provide useful leverage without requiring a knowledge platform.

---

# 5. Promotion 3 — Risk & Failure Check

## Decision

**PROMOTE**

Project OS v0.1 will include a proportionate Risk & Failure Check.

## Purpose

Counter the tendency to design and generate only the successful path.

## Questions may include

- What can fail?
- What can be entered incorrectly?
- What can become stale?
- What can partially succeed?
- What external dependency can fail?
- What happens over time?
- How is the action reversed or corrected?
- What data can be lost?
- Who owns recovery?
- What evidence is required that the action completed?

## Evidence

- Scorekeeper required correction and undo.
- Rivervalley failed at the DNS layer despite healthy application code.
- IMS contained rejection, expiry, revocation, partial automation and hand-off risk.
- Scribble's local-only architecture introduced recovery and backup trade-offs.

## Important constraint

This is not a mandatory enterprise risk assessment.

A weekend utility and an enterprise exception workflow should not receive the same review depth.

## v0.1 hypothesis

A short negative-path review at the right moment will identify disproportionately valuable issues before they become expensive.

---

# 6. Promotion 4 — Scope & Stop Check

## Decision

**PROMOTE**

Project OS v0.1 will include a Scope & Stop Check.

This is the first implementation of the evidence behind **The Brake**.

## Purpose

Detect when productive work is becoming unnecessary iteration, accumulation or abstraction.

## Questions may include

- Are we still solving the original problem?
- Has scope expanded without an explicit decision?
- Are we polishing before direction is settled?
- Are we building methodology instead of the product?
- Are plausible features accumulating simply because they can?
- Are we adding AI because it is available rather than necessary?
- Are we restructuring working software without sufficient reason?
- Is another review materially changing the result?
- Are we deepening the product promise or expanding it?
- What happens if we stop and ship what we have?

## Evidence

- Lucy: Polish Event Horizon and Process Event Horizon.
- Eolas: risk of knowledge collection becoming the work.
- Rivervalley: unnecessary post-release changes can threaten working software.
- Ground Control: modular scope accumulation.
- Scribble: feature expansion, metaphor abuse and AI-by-default risk.

## Important constraint

The Brake is promoted as a **capability**, not as an autonomous agent.

No `agents/the-brake.md` should be created merely because the concept has a name.

## v0.1 hypothesis

A lightweight checkpoint will prevent more waste than a continuously active process-control agent.

That hypothesis should be tested.

---

# 7. Promotion 5 — Learning Capture

## Decision

**PROMOTE**

Project OS v0.1 will include lightweight Learning Capture.

## Purpose

Ensure meaningful project experience can benefit future work.

## Capture candidates

Only material learning should be considered, such as:

- a decision whose reasoning is likely to matter again;
- an approach that failed and why;
- a production incident and its resolution;
- an unexpected user behaviour;
- a reusable pattern;
- an anti-pattern;
- a technical constraint;
- a trade-off validated in practice.

## Evidence

- Eolas established the original knowledge-retention problem.
- Rivervalley showed the value of retaining incident knowledge.
- IMS showed the value of retaining workflow failure and governance lessons.
- Scorekeeper showed that real usage exposes requirements not obvious in design.
- Lucy showed that failed design/process approaches can be more reusable than final UI details.

## Important constraint

Learning Capture must not become indiscriminate collection.

A project should not generate documentation simply because something happened.

The test is:

> **Could this materially improve a future decision?**

## v0.1 hypothesis

Small, provenance-rich learning records will be more useful than large unqualified knowledge collections.

# 8. Supporting concerns accepted into v0.1

The five capabilities above are the implementation focus.

The following synthesis findings are accepted as **behavioural constraints** on them rather than separate capabilities.

## 8.1 Adaptive process depth

Project OS must scale its intervention to the project.

No capability is automatically applied at maximum depth.

---

## 8.2 Context before intervention

Project OS should understand existing behaviour, systems and constraints before recommending replacement or redesign.

---

## 8.3 Complexity containment

Necessary complexity should be contained.

Accidental complexity should be challenged.

---

## 8.4 Protect working systems

Existing working behaviour must be understood before substantial restructuring.

Generated replacement code is not evidence that replacement is safe.

---

## 8.5 Lifecycle awareness

Where time or state materially changes behaviour, lifecycle should be made explicit.

This does not require formal state modelling for every project.

---

# 9. Retained but not promoted

The following remain useful evidence but do not become Project OS v0.1 capabilities.

## Product Metaphor

Retain as a potential product/design technique.

Use when it materially reduces ambiguity.

Do not require it.

## Low-friction Capture

Retain as a reusable interaction/product pattern.

It is not a general operating-system concern.

## Responsibility Mapping

Retain for projects with meaningful human/system hand-offs.

Potential future risk-triggered skill.

## Accessibility by Construction

Retain as an important quality concern.

The archaeology has not yet established its Project OS implementation form.

## Privacy/Security Proportionality

Retain as a likely risk-triggered review capability.

Do not create a universal security ceremony from current evidence.

---

# 10. Explore, but do not implement in v0.1

## Project Council

Promising, but insufficient evidence that a formal council materially improves enough decisions to justify its cost.

A deliberate experiment is required.

## Autonomous Brake agent

The capability is promoted.

The agent form is not.

## Permanent Design Director

Useful in Lucy-like uncertainty.

Not demonstrated as universally useful.

## Eolas architecture

The knowledge problem is proven.

The storage/retrieval architecture is not.

## AI Resource Strategy

Remain on the roadmap.

We do not yet have sufficient evidence about model routing, reasoning levels, cost or multi-model combinations.

## Multi-model orchestration

No evidence currently justifies adding this to v0.1.

---

# 11. Explicitly rejected for v0.1

Project OS v0.1 will **not** include:

- a universal technology stack;
- universal UI rules;
- a mandatory Project Council;
- a mandatory Design Director;
- a permanent Brake agent;
- a multi-agent orchestration framework;
- a vector knowledge database;
- automatic repository ingestion;
- automated model routing;
- token/credit optimisation;
- reusable internal packages;
- a project bootstrap CLI;
- dozens of skills;
- dozens of agents;
- formal scoring machinery;
- automated process-drift detection;
- a large mandatory lifecycle.

These may be reconsidered only when evidence changes.

# 12. v0.1 operating model

The first working Project OS experiment is:

```text
                    ┌────────────────────┐
                    │   PROJECT INTAKE   │
                    └─────────┬──────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
                    ┌────────────────────┐
                    │ RELEVANT KNOWLEDGE │
                    │       CHECK        │
                    └─────────┬──────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
                       DECIDE / BUILD
                         │         │
              ┌──────────┘         └──────────┐
              ▼                               ▼
    ┌────────────────────┐          ┌────────────────────┐
    │   RISK & FAILURE   │          │   SCOPE & STOP     │
    │       CHECK        │          │       CHECK        │
    └────────────────────┘          └────────────────────┘
                         │         │
                         └────┬────┘
                              ▼
                    ┌────────────────────┐
                    │  LEARNING CAPTURE  │
                    └────────────────────┘
```

This diagram represents available capabilities, not a rigid sequence.

The Scope & Stop Check can occur at any point.

The Risk & Failure Check should occur when the consequence of failure justifies it.

Learning Capture should occur when there is something worth retaining.

---

# 13. How v0.1 should be tested

Project OS v0.1 should now be used on **one real project**.

The test should not be:

> Can we create polished Project OS documents?

It should be:

> **Does using these five capabilities improve the project enough to justify their cost?**

For each capability, record:

- Was it used?
- Why was it activated?
- What did it change?
- What useful issue did it surface?
- How much friction did it create?
- Would the project have been materially worse without it?

The experiment should also record when Project OS correctly chose **not** to intervene.

That is important evidence.

---

# 14. Success criteria

Project OS v0.1 is successful if, during a real project, it demonstrates several of the following without becoming burdensome:

- prevents an unnecessary decision from being reinvented;
- exposes a material assumption before implementation;
- identifies a failure/recovery issue early;
- prevents scope or design drift;
- prevents unnecessary restructuring of working software;
- retrieves a useful previous lesson;
- captures a new lesson worth reusing;
- correctly applies less process to a low-risk decision;
- helps the project reach a stopping point sooner.

It does not need to demonstrate every item in one project.

---

# 15. Failure criteria

v0.1 should be reconsidered if it:

- creates more documentation than useful decisions;
- repeatedly interrupts obvious work;
- produces generic advice;
- requires substantial maintenance;
- duplicates what the coding agent already does well;
- encourages more discussion instead of progress;
- turns every project into a governance exercise;
- becomes another object of continuous design and optimisation.

The last failure is particularly important.

Project OS itself remains subject to the Scope & Stop Check.

---

# 16. Implementation constraint

The next step is **not** to create five sophisticated agents.

The first implementation should be intentionally primitive.

Prefer:

- concise Markdown;
- a small number of prompts/checkpoints;
- existing repository context;
- manual invocation;
- visible evidence of why each capability ran.

Avoid automation until repetition demonstrates where automation would remove real work.

---

# 17. Decision consequences

## Positive

Project OS now has a bounded v0.1 scope grounded in seven projects.

The project can move from research into experimentation without inventing a full operating framework.

The five capabilities cover the strongest recurring problems found during archaeology.

## Negative

v0.1 will initially require some manual judgement.

It will not automatically know which previous knowledge is relevant.

It will not automatically route work to specialised agents or models.

It may feel less sophisticated than systems that begin with extensive orchestration.

This is intentional.

## Risk

The largest remaining risk is that the five lightweight capabilities gradually expand into large subsystems before they have demonstrated value.

The Scope & Stop Check must therefore be applied to Project OS itself.

---

# 18. Decision

**Accepted.**

Project OS v0.1 consists of:

1. Project Intake
2. Relevant Knowledge Check
3. Risk & Failure Check
4. Scope & Stop Check
5. Learning Capture

All other candidate capabilities remain retained, exploratory or rejected according to this ADR.

No further capability should enter v0.1 without evidence that the current five are insufficient.

---

## Next action

Build the **smallest usable representation** of these five capabilities and run them against one real project.

Do not automate them yet.

Do not add another capability yet.

Do not resume archaeology without a specific evidence gap.

The next design question is therefore:

> **What is the minimum form these five capabilities need in order to be genuinely usable during a project?**
